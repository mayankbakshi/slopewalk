# Changelog

## Unreleased

- `theme="auto"` is the default: the picture and the controls follow the editor's theme where the kernel can read it on the same machine (VS Code's current theme, which VS Code records for its start-up screen; JupyterLab's or Notebook 7's theme setting). Elsewhere, Colab included, they are light. `SLOPEWALK_THEME=dark` or `light` chooses for every notebook, and the switch still changes both.
- The dark palette has higher contrast: near-black paper (#0D1117), brighter ink, class colours and negative weights, and box edges, frames and table lines that stand out. Quiet text in the dark controls is less faded.
- In VS Code a dark picture no longer sits in a white frame: VS Code puts every widget output on white, and the output that holds the controls now takes the picture's paper. The row of numbers in the status is no longer shaded by VS Code's table style.
- With several classes, the legend of the input plane marks a class that is the most likely nowhere ("class 2: no region"), so that a class missing from the picture is not taken for a drawing error.
- More than two classes: a network that ends with `nn.Linear(..., K)` (K from 2 to 4) has one score per class, the labels are 0 to K-1, and R is the cross-entropy, as `F.cross_entropy`. The task follows from the outputs (`task="multiclass"`). Each network panel shows the scores a0, a1, ..., one softmax box, p for each class and the loss; the p of the group's own class has its class colours, since the loss takes it. Classes 2 and 3 are teal and magenta, hues that nothing else in the picture uses. The input plane shows the most likely class, shaded by how sure the network is, with the boundaries between the classes; the views, the backward pass (δak = pk − yk), the accuracy and the slider groups follow. With one output, labels such as 2 now get an error that says how many outputs the network needs.
- In the Worked example, a sum over three or more next units is written two terms to a line, so that the lines stay in their column. At the output stage of a regression network, the hint now says δa = 2(ŷ − y) instead of p − y.
- The slope table fits its columns to their width: in a narrow block (more than 15 weights in the standard layout) each column gets the share it needs and the headers get shorter, instead of numbers running into each other.
- The controls take the colours of the picture: with `theme="dark"` or the dark switch they are dark too (background, text, buttons, sliders, tabs, drop-down lists), whatever the notebook's own theme; with light, they are light. The colours come from the picture's palette.
- The panel of the backward pass is headed "Worked example" in the colour of the backward pass, with the case after it and the stage on the line below; the case picker is called Worked example too.
- Dark mode: `explore(..., theme="dark")` and a light/dark switch under the controls draw the picture in light ink on dark paper (#1E1E1E). Every colour of the picture is now in `Theme` (a test fails on any colour written elsewhere), and both palettes are checked: text at 4.5:1 or more on every surface it sits on, lines at 3:1, and every text a picture shows at 3:1 or more against its background.
- A calmer palette: class colours #0969DA and #CF222E, ink #1F2328, negative weights #D1600A. Text in boxes is ink, and a box carries its meaning in its border and fill; green marks the hidden layer only.
- Larger text below the networks: the table at up to 12 pt and the worked example and notes at 11 pt, made smaller only when they would not fit; panel titles 12 pt, legends 9.5 pt, tick labels 10.5 pt.
- In the backpropagation view the names of the values (z, h, a, p, δ, ∂ℓ/∂h and the slopes' names) are written in panel A only; the other panels show the values. The tab is called Backpropagation, and the case picker and the text panel are called the worked example.
- Changes from a second design review. Lines always show the sign of their weight; a gradient step no longer turns them blue, so blue means class 0 only. A weight moved by hand since the last step has a bold label in the first panel, and the one moved last keeps its name. Panels after the first say "weights as in A". The status shows the change of R in the last action (a step, ten steps, back one step or a slider move) with a note on what it was. The R curve's axis fits the values, so progress shows. The four buttons wrap together in a narrow window. Each view's settings sit beside its checkbox and are disabled while the view is off. Group letters have a white outline, so they stay legible over clustered points. The backward-pass hint says "the text panel below the networks".
- Changes from a design review. The status shows R and the change of R in the last step in large type, with the other numbers smaller, and keeps the same columns before and after the first step. Text in the controls takes the colours of the notebook's theme, so dark themes work; the green and red of the change keep at least 3.9:1 on white and on dark backgrounds. A `back one step` button and `Session.step_back()` undo the last gradient step. The buttons are disabled while the picture is drawn, and previous stage and next stage stop at the ends. All sliders apply when released. The layout switch moved to the end of the controls, and the caption under the backward-pass buttons was dropped (the picture's panel title carries the stage). The first message is empty, and the hint is one sentence.
- In the picture: the weights carry their numbers in the first network panel only, since they are the same in every panel; the slopes of the backward pass, which differ from case to case, stay in every panel. The weight moved last with a slider keeps its name on its line. Class 1 is a darker red (#C62828: 5.6:1 on white, 4.7:1 on its tint) and the lines of negative weights a darker orange (#E86A00: 3.2:1 on white), and lines are at least 1 pt wide. One title size for the panels below the networks, one legend size and one letter size, all in `Theme`. Momentum is dashed, so it differs from gradient descent by more than colour. R has 4 decimals in the picture as in the status, the backward-pass panel counts stages ("stage 2 of 6"), and "eta 0.5" is written the same way everywhere.
- The wide layout uses 10% larger text in the network panels, with rows and the loss box moved to make room; where the slope labels of the backward pass do not fit at 3 decimals, they are shown with 2 instead of being dropped. With groups of points, the boxes of the backward pass sit low enough to leave the standard deviation line above them readable. A segment break in the path of the steps now marks only a slider move, not the float32 rounding of the network's weights.
- The picture comes first, with a status line and the controls under it. The controls: one line on what to do, a row with eta, the step buttons and the layout switch, and the sliders in a column per unit. Tabs appear only for what the call asks for (Backprop when `views` includes "backprop", Views for other views), and changing tabs leaves the picture as it is. The status line gives the numbers and one sentence on what the last action did; with the backward pass, it says where to look. Every control has a tooltip. The default layout is `"auto"`: wide where the sliders run in Jupyter or VS Code, standard in Colab and for still pictures.
- Backpropagation step by step: `views="backprop"` (or `backprop_step=k`) draws the backward pass stage by stage, from δ at the output through the slopes of the output weights, ∂ℓ/∂h, δ through the activation (with its tangent) and the slopes of each earlier layer, to the average over the cases. A text panel writes out the numbers for one case (`backprop_case`); the widgets step through the stages; `Session.record_backprop` writes them to a GIF. Every value is computed with the taught formulas and checked against autograd.
- `layout="wide"` and `width`: a figure 27 inches wide with up to four network panels and six lower panels per row. Each lower panel gets a width that suits it, and every row is filled. Narrow tables use short headers.
- The network panels keep the same size and spacing whatever panels are below them.
- New options of `explore`, all keyword-only, so existing calls are unchanged: `task="regression"` (mean squared error, a prediction box instead of the sigmoid and p, the fitted curve or surface in the input panel), `params` (the weights that move; the others are drawn lighter), `views` (extra views: each case's slope along one weight, R over one or two weights with the path of the steps, R along the next step with its first-order prediction, the cases in the hidden layer, and gradient descent against momentum), `case_slopes`, `slice_weights`, `beta`, `lookahead` and `log_scale`.
- The first-order prediction -eta times the sum of the squared slopes is recorded for every step and shown next to the actual change: in the history panel, in its note, and in a new readout strip of the widgets.
- The starting boundary is drawn dashed once the weights have moved; a network with one input shows p against x1.
- `Session.record(path, steps)` writes a GIF of a run (MP4 when ffmpeg is installed). `Session.predict`, `lookahead`, `case_slopes` and `readouts` give the numbers behind the panels.
- Clear errors for points and networks that slopewalk cannot use: NaN or inf, wrong shapes or lengths, labels other than 0 and 1, `bias=False`, half precision or mixed precision, networks off the CPU. float64 networks work throughout.
- A step that takes a weight past its slider's end widens the slider instead of clipping the weight; sliders start wider around weights beyond ±4; `step()` called in code updates the sliders.
- The widgets keep one figure and send it as a PNG to an image widget: a slider move redraws in about 200 ms instead of about 380 ms for the 13-weight example, without flicker. `SLOPEWALK_DISPLAY=output` shows the picture through `display` instead.
- Label placement is vectorised (the same positions, about 30 times faster) and runs after the layout of the figure; rows and activation glyphs adapt to up to 12 units per layer, and long tables use two taller blocks.
- `sine_points()`: a regression example with one input.

- Renamed from gradviz to slopewalk and split into a package (`src/slopewalk`): model, data, layout, draw, widgets, static, session.
- `explore` returns a `Session` with `save(path)`, `step(k)`, `reset()`, `weights()` and `history`. Nothing else changed in what it draws or computes.
- New `weight_table(net)`: name, layer, unit, input and value of every weight.
- `slopes` no longer touches the `.grad` of the network's parameters, and keeps their `requires_grad` flags.
- Edge labels are placed so that they overlap neither each other nor the boxes: each label tries 13 places along its line and small shifts to either side, and the figure degrades from names and values, to values only, to no labels (the values are then in the table and on the sliders). A test checks every example for overlaps.
- Panel rows grow with the number of units in a layer (up to twice the height) and by a fifth for groups of points.
- The table also lists the value of every weight.
- The slope panel is a table with a title, a header per column (weight, change of R for +0.1, slope dR/dw) and the steepest row highlighted. Above 15 weights it continues in a second block.
- `dist/slopewalk_single.py`: the package in one file, for pasting into a notebook cell (`tools/make_single_file.py`).
- Tests, ruff and GitHub Actions (Python 3.9 with the oldest supported versions, 3.12 with Colab's versions, 3.13 with the newest).
